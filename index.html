About me

I'm a PhD candidate in physics at West Virginia University, working with Sean T. McWilliams on gravitational and high energy astrophysics.

My research uses numerical and analytical methods to study gravitational waves, black holes on hyperbolic orbits, kinetic plasma effects around black holes, and more. This involves using general relativistic particle-in-cell simulations (Entity), numerical relativity simulations (Einstein Toolkit), and analytical black hole perturbation theory!

I expect to complete my PhD in May 2027.

2026submitted · PRL

The Direct Wave is Not a Meaningful Test of Horizon Properties

A. Kankani, S. T. McWilliams

Using numerical-relativity strain data, we show that the direct wave's frequency does not track the remnant's horizon frequency or surface gravity. Using it to test Hawking's area law (as done for GW250114) can signal violations where none exist.

arXiv:2607.02380
2026submitted · PRD

Direct Waves in Black-Hole Binary Mergers: Insights from the Backwards One Body Model

A. Kankani, S. T. McWilliams

Explains why the BOB model is so accurate near merger: a Pöschl–Teller analysis links quasinormal-mode poles to BOB's amplitude profile, and rational filters show BOB naturally captures the non-QNM "direct wave" — a component largely uncorrelated with the horizon, even at high spin.

arXiv:2603.15474
2026submitted · JOSS

gwBOB: A Python Package for Analytical Merger–Ringdown Gravitational Waveforms

A. Kankani, A. Morales, S. Dasgupta, S. T. McWilliams

An open-source Python package (which I lead) implementing the Backwards One Body model, with utilities for generating merger–ringdown waveforms and comparing them directly against numerical relativity.

2025submitted · PRD

BOB the (Waveform) Builder: Optimizing Analytical Black-Hole Binary Merger Waveforms

A. Kankani, S. T. McWilliams

A comprehensive validation of the fully analytical BOB model for the dominant (2,2) mode, showing it reaches accuracy comparable to highly calibrated effective-one-body and NR surrogate models — while staying accurate in sparsely-sampled regions of parameter space because it is minimally tuned.

arXiv:2510.25012
2025Phys. Rev. D 112, 124051

Modeling Relative Peak Times of Gravitational Wave Harmonics

A. Kankani, S. T. McWilliams

Two semi-analytical methods — one from BOB's frequency evolution, one from a timelike geodesic in the remnant spacetime — that predict when each harmonic mode peaks, matching the SXS catalog to within about 1 M for l = |m| modes up to l = 8.

arXiv:2506.17492
2024Phys. Rev. D 110, 064033

Testing the Boundary-to-Bound Correspondence With Numerical Relativity

A. Kankani, S. T. McWilliams

Runs a large suite of numerical-relativity simulations of bound and unbound black-hole encounters to test the boundary-to-bound correspondence beyond the perturbative regime, finding that the relationships break down across a wide range of eccentricities — a clear limit on where they apply.

arXiv:2404.03607
2021Phys. Rev. E 104, 035302

Optimization and Benchmarking of the Thermal Cycling Algorithm

A. Barzegar, A. Kankani, S. Mandrà, H. G. Katzgraber

An undergraduate project tuning and benchmarking the thermal cycling optimization algorithm, showing it competes closely with parallel tempering using isoenergetic cluster moves while far outperforming simpler heuristics like simulated annealing.

arXiv:2012.09801 · Phys. Rev. E
