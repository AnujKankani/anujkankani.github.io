About me

I'm a PhD candidate in physics at West Virginia University, working with Sean T. McWilliams on the theory and modeling of gravitational waves from black-hole binaries.

My work develops fast, analytical merger–ringdown waveform models, uses numerical relativity to test strong-field predictions of general relativity, and simulates the relativistic plasmas that surround compact objects. I care about models that are both physically grounded and useful to the people analyzing real data — from LIGO today to LISA in the coming decade.

I expect to complete my PhD in 2027.

Research

Three threads, one goal: describing gravity where it's strongest.

01

Analytical Waveform Modeling

Building and optimizing the Backwards One Body (BOB) model for merger–ringdown gravitational waves, extending it beyond the dominant mode, and releasing it as the open-source gwBOB package for the community.

02

Tests of Strong-Field Gravity

Using numerical relativity to probe what waveforms can and cannot tell us — testing the boundary-to-bound correspondence, and asking whether the "direct wave" is a meaningful test of black-hole horizon properties.

03

Relativistic Plasma Astrophysics

General Relativistic Particle-in-Cell (GRPIC) simulations of the magnetospheres and jets of black holes, including what drives temperature anisotropies in relativistic jets.

The Direct Wave is Not a Meaningful Test of Horizon Properties

A. Kankani, S. T. McWilliams

Using numerical-relativity strain data, shows that the direct wave's frequency does not track the remnant's horizon frequency or surface gravity — so using it to test Hawking's area law (as done for GW250114) can signal violations where none exist.

Direct Waves in Black-Hole Binary Mergers: Insights from the Backwards One Body Model

A. Kankani, S. T. McWilliams

Explains why the BOB model is so accurate near merger: a Pöschl–Teller analysis links quasinormal-mode poles to BOB's amplitude profile, and rational filters show BOB naturally captures the non-QNM "direct wave" — a component largely uncorrelated with the horizon, even at high spin.

A. Kankani, S. T. McWilliams

Two semi-analytical methods — one from BOB's frequency evolution, one from a timelike geodesic in the remnant spacetime — that predict when each harmonic mode peaks, matching the SXS catalog to within about 1 M for l = |m| modes up to l = 8.

Merger-Ringdown Modeling of Gravitational Waves Beyond the Dominant Mode

BOB

The Backwards One Body model, in a minute

Most merger models push forwards from an inspiral and fit a ringdown on the end. BOB starts from what the system must become — a single perturbed black hole — and works backwards. The merger waveform then follows from the physics, with almost nothing tuned to numerical relativity.

Amplitude

A bundle of null rays perturbed off the light ring diverges at the Lyapunov rate. Carrying that divergence through the transport equation gives a sech envelope — no free parameters.

Frequency

The angular-momentum flux is quadratic in the News, and dJ/dΩ stays roughly constant through merger–ringdown. Substituting the amplitude and integrating gives Ω, rising monotonically to the ringdown value.
