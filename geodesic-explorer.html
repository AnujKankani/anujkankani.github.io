A massive particle released from rest (radially) at r₀ falls inward and either settles into a precessing bound orbit — the relativistic rosette that never quite closes — or, if its angular momentum is too low to clear the potential barrier, plunges through the horizon. The innermost stable circular orbit sits at r = 6M. A photon is deflected as it passes; below the critical impact parameter b = 3√3 M ≈ 5.2M it is captured, and near it light can whirl around the photon sphere at r = 3M before escaping.
